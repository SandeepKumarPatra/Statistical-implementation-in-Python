A Normal Distribution (also called Gaussian Distribution) is a probability
distribution where data forms a symmetrical bell-shaped curve.
Defined by two parameters

Mean (μ) → center of data (avg)
Standard deviation (σ) → how spread out the data is
Standard Deviation is a measure that tells how spread out the data is 
from the mean (average).

1.CLT   Central Limit Theorem (CLT)
The CLT states that When we take many random samples of sufficiently 
large size (usually n ≥ 30) from any population, the distribution of the 
sample means will approximate a normal distribution, regardless of the population’s
original distribution.

Population
↓
Sample (30 people) → mean₁
Sample (30 people) → mean₂
Sample (30 people) → mean₃
Sample (30 people) → mean₄
...
Many sample means
↓
Distribution of means ≈ Normal

2.CI   Confidence Interval (CI)
A range of values that likely contains the true population parameter
with a specified level of confidence.
Formula: CI = x̄ ± Z × (σ/√n)
Suppose you want the average height of all students in a city.
You take a sample of 100 students.
Sample mean:
x̄ = 170 cm
After applying the formula, you get:
Confidence Interval = 168 cm to 172 cm

3.Bootstrapping etc
A resampling technique that estimates a statistic's distribution by 
repeatedly sampling with replacement from your own data — no assumptions
 about the population needed.
 ex
 Original Dataset
[10, 20, 30, 40, 50]
Pick → 10
Because this is sampling with replacement, we put 10 back into the dataset.
So the dataset does NOT change.
Dataset is still:
[10, 20, 30, 40, 50]
again pick it may 10 again
